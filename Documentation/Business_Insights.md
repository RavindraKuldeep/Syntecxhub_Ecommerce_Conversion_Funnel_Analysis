# Business Insights

## 1. High Drop-off at Product Discovery

Only 22.70% of visitors progress from the Visit stage to Product View.

This represents a 77.30% drop-off at the first major funnel transition.

### Business Implication

Product discovery and product-page engagement require attention.

---

## 2. Product View to Add to Cart is a Major Bottleneck

The conversion from Product View to Add to Cart is 20.48%.

The corresponding drop-off is 79.52%.

### Business Implication

Users are viewing products but a large proportion are not adding them to the cart.

---

## 3. Cart to Purchase Shows Stronger Conversion

The Add to Cart → Purchase conversion is 35.23%.

This is the strongest stage-to-stage conversion rate among the three funnel transitions.

### Business Implication

Once users reach the cart stage, they are comparatively more likely to complete the purchase.

---

## 4. Early Funnel Stages Need Attention

The largest volume of users is lost before reaching the cart stage.

The analysis therefore highlights product discovery, product-page engagement and Add to Cart as important areas for improvement.

---

## 5. Recommended Actions

- Improve product-page user experience.
- Make Add to Cart calls-to-action more visible.
- Improve product information and pricing visibility.
- Use relevant product recommendations.
- Investigate friction between product view and cart.
- Monitor monthly funnel conversion trends.
